## Overview

This research program investigates multi-decadal glacier change and dynamics across **~4,000 glaciers on the Kenai Peninsula, Alaska**. Spanning observations from **1986 to 2019**, it connects changes in glacier geometry and mass with variations in ice motion, atmospheric forcing, and meltwater runoff.

The work integrates optical satellite imagery, synthetic aperture radar, stereo photogrammetry, satellite altimetry, climate data, and glacier-model outputs. It progresses from regional assessments of glacier area and mass change to analyses of surface-velocity variability and glacier speed–runoff relationships.

## Key Contributions

- Developed regional glacier-outline and elevation products to quantify long-term changes in glacier area, surface elevation, and mass.
- Generated glacier-velocity fields and assessed their spatial and temporal variability across the Kenai Peninsula.
- Investigated relationships among glacier motion, atmospheric forcing, and meltwater runoff by integrating satellite observations with model-derived data.
- Built reproducible workflows for processing and combining heterogeneous optical, radar, altimetry, climate, and glacier-model datasets.
- Produced **three peer-reviewed publications and three published regional datasets** supporting validation, comparative analysis, and further research.

## Research Progression

### Glacier Area and Mass Change

The first phase assessed regional glacier area and mass change between **1986 and 2016**. Glacier outlines were derived from multi-temporal satellite imagery, while digital elevation models generated from ASTER stereo observations supported estimates of surface-elevation and mass change.

This work established a consistent regional record of glacier retreat and mass loss across the Kenai Peninsula.

### Glacier Surface-Velocity Variability

The second phase mapped glacier surface velocity from **2014 to 2019** using satellite radar observations and offset-tracking methods.

The resulting regional velocity products were used to examine spatial differences among glaciers and temporal variations in ice motion.

### Glacier Speed and Runoff

The third phase investigated how seasonal glacier-speed variations relate to atmospheric forcing and meltwater runoff.

By combining remotely sensed velocity measurements with modeled runoff, the study advanced regional-scale understanding of glacier-dynamic responses to seasonal hydrological forcing.
